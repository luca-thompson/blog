# The Durrels' House

A scene rather worthy of brush and canvas, this view before him was. Standing on tip-toe and leaning slightly too far over the sill one could, as he was now, see just past the chimney enough to take in the whole of the Durrels' hilltop livings. It was always rather picturesque; but today, in this specific moment, it had something more; each part of the ensemble was seemingly meticulously selected and put in place such as to incite something in its audience. Spring was to be credited first for both heavens and breeze. Clouds were present, but only in their sunday best and on the best of behaviors, speaking of nothing other than a smattering of shade and daring not even a whisper of rain. Further to seasonal attribution was the newly invigorated sun, woken from its wintery torpor to once again find stable footing in the sky and shine with a missed warmth. As match a slight breeze was about, enough to brush the cheeks of those sweating in the adjustment period and cool them still. From here however it was the Durrels who must be given praise, for they too were aiding in the scene. Their last-century wooden house was portly but prideful in age; one imagined inside it was without the haughty ceilings endemic to modern city homes and instead featuerd the low intimate ones of more rustic abodes. Built by the now dead Arthur Durrell's own hands some seventy years ago, when the family had first come, it had a right to pride; housing two generations, being the site of likely every human emotion under the sun; it had likely seen much of what there is to be seen of humanity. However all of this somehow paled in comparison to the real crux of the scene; some four or five (it is difficult to tell from such a distance) pieces of washng—bed sheets likely—were hung up and out. Matching the natural off-white of the clouds they wavered in the breeze ever so slightly, shimmerring under bright sun. It was this, the simplicity of the act—of the object and its use, that made it move something inside oneself. Base to life are the chores that enable its most fundamental parts, using our most plain creations.
