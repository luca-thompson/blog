# Two Rooms

And, like that, a door is closed. Each room cut off from the other, nothing more than flickers of light sneaking through the keyhole and whispers worming their way under the door. It isn't locked, neither side has barred the other from opening the door at all. It may as well be locked though, for both sides know the other would not, and possibly could not, bring themselves to even try the latch for some time. It wasn't shut without cause after all. And anyway, could you imagine the self-loathing brought on by an attempt at turning the handle, if that attempt was met with the click of a bolt set by key?

At one time, neither room could even tell the door was there; everything flowed between them unfettered. There wasn't a taste, cry, song, jest, jumper or idea that found obstruction as it passed the threshold from one to the other. Each room found itself conforming and stretching with the influence of the other; carpets changing to floorboards, new wallpapers and strangely shaped furniture appearing. Each used the other as muse and cause for change. The rooms grew with eachother, adding bends and strange dividers, trying different layouts of their decorations. Things were torn down, replaced, re-torn down and put back up after a second thought. Somehow their makeup was differing too, what was once thin drywall was now morphed and solidifyed into insulating brick. They helped eachother too, on cold nights if a heater, or perhaps more likely the will to switch it on, was broken it took only a little time for the warm air of one to flow into the other. The guests of each rooms profited from this openess too; jokes found larger runways on which to land and sobs were met with more sympathy and understanding than ever.

Maybe with times passing, in a  far-far future, a tentative hand shall pluck up with courage and try the door. If the handle does turn, and no bolt has been set, it might be opened again. It would be slow; the hinges would be quite rusty, the  mechanisms in the handle possibly seized some. Regardless one imagines a grand discussion, broad and wistful as it meanders down the trails of their memories, for the rooms would have much to reminisce about. They might laugh about the ridiculous wallpaper one of them sported and cry about the way a once-guest spilt red wine on their new rug. Yes, it would have to be a very broad conversation indeed.
