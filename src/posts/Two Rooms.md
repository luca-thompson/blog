# Two Rooms

And, like that, a door is closed. Each room cut off from the other, nothing more than flickers of light sneaking through the keyhole and whispers worming their way under the door made it through now. It wasn't locked, neither side had barred the other from opening the door at all. It may as well be locked though, for both sides new the other would not, and possibly could not, bring themselves to even try the latch for some time. It wasn't shut without cause after all. And anyway, could you imagine the self-loathing inflicted by an attempt at turning the handle met with the click of a lock?

At one time, neither room could even tell the door was there; everything flowed between them unfettered. Jokes found larger audiences, sobs were met with broader understanding and a great kinship formed. Music played in one room reached the other with no trouble, where its guests listened with great interest. Books were passed between the rooms as eaches guests took more and more to the others tastes. On particularly cold nights if a heater was broken in one it was of little concern for the warmth of one could flow unobstructed to the other. 

Maybe with times passing, in a  far-far future, a tentative hand shall pluck up with courage and try the door. If the handle does turn, and no bolt has been set, the door might be opened again. It would be slow; the hinges might be quite rusty with disuse by then, perhaps so much so the door can't quite open all the way. One imagines a grand discussion full of wistful laughing and perhaps a melancholy tear here or there, for the rooms would have much to reminisce about. They might decide that, in hindsight and with whatever they have learned in the meantime, shutting the door quite so abruptly mightn't have been the best way to go about things.
